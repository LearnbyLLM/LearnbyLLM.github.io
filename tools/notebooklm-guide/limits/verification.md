# What to Always Verify

A quick reference for what needs human eyes.

---

## Always Verify

These should never be trusted from NotebookLM without checking:

### Financial Figures

- [ ] Revenue numbers
- [ ] Profit/loss figures
- [ ] Margins and percentages
- [ ] Cash flow amounts
- [ ] Debt levels
- [ ] Share counts
- [ ] Per-share figures
- [ ] Any number in a table

### Time-Sensitive Data

- [ ] Which fiscal year/quarter a figure belongs to
- [ ] Date ranges for metrics
- [ ] When guidance was given
- [ ] Filing dates vs. period dates

### Names and Titles

- [ ] Executive names and positions
- [ ] Board member roles
- [ ] Company names (subsidiaries, competitors mentioned)

### Specific Amounts

- [ ] Contract values
- [ ] Acquisition prices
- [ ] Settlement amounts
- [ ] Customer percentages

---

## Usually Safe (But Spot-Check)

These are typically reliable, but click a few citations to confirm:

### Qualitative Information

- Business descriptions
- Strategy narratives
- Risk factor categories
- Competitive positioning statements

### Structural Information

- What sections exist
- General themes
- What topics are discussed

### Direct Quotes

When you ask for exact quotes, NotebookLM is usually accurate. Still worth one click to verify.

---

## Quick Verification Workflow

1. **Get the answer** from NotebookLM
2. **Note any numbers** or specific claims
3. **Click the citation** for each important claim
4. **Open original PDF** to the cited location
5. **Confirm** what the source actually says
6. **Use verified information** in your analysis

Takes 30 seconds per important claim. Worth it.

---

## Red Flags to Watch For

Be extra skeptical when NotebookLM:

- **Gives round numbers** — Real financials usually aren't round
- **Combines figures** — "Total revenue of X plus Y equals Z" — check the math
- **Compares across tables** — High chance of column confusion
- **Reports percentages** — Often miscalculated or from wrong period
- **Makes comparative statements** — "Higher than last year" — verify both numbers
- **No citation provided** — Ask it to cite or be very skeptical

---

## When It Matters Most

Verification is especially critical when:

- Making actual investment decisions
- Sharing information with others
- Writing anything that others will rely on
- Numbers will be used in models or calculations
- The answer seems surprising (good or bad)

---

## The 30-Second Rule

If you're about to use any specific fact from NotebookLM:

> Spend 30 seconds clicking the citation and verifying against the original source.

That's it. One habit. Saves embarrassment and money.
